Research Experience

Doctoral Researcher — Knowledge Tracing Interpretability 2025 – present
Department of Computer Science, New Mexico State University
  • Evaluating whether knowledge tracing models that claim interpretability — in particular QIKT (AAAI 2023) and IKT (AAAI 2022) — are faithful at the level of individual students, using the pykt-toolkit benchmarking framework on the ASSIST2009 dataset.
  • Designed and ran three original experimental studies: cold-start behavior of the model's interpretability scores, the empirical semantics of its three internal prediction streams, and assumption-breaking tests based on controlled edits to student response sequences.
  • Showed that the model's named interpretability scores are largely redundant, and that its explanations are unstable during a student's early interactions even though predictive accuracy (AUC) remains stable.
  • Built a pipeline that generates teacher-facing explanations from the model's internal scores and validates LLM-generated explanations through hallucination checks, faithfulness grading against measured correlation ceilings, and forbidden-claim detection.
  • Reproduced the MSKT metacognitive knowledge tracing model on the EdNet-KT4 dataset, obtaining results close to those reported in the original paper.
  • Independently identified an implementation inconsistency in the published QIKT source code and overparameterization in one of its modules.
  • Experiments run on a multi-GPU Linux cluster (NVIDIA H100) using PyTorch and conda environments.
M.Sc. Research — Sparse Representation for Face Recognition 2013 – 2016
Tarbiat Modares University, Tehran, Iran
  • Developed and evaluated a face recognition method based on sparse representation, combining dictionary-based classification with clustering techniques.
